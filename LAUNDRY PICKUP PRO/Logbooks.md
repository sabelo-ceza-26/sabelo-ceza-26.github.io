#### REFLECTION

**Situation:**
Between 14 July and 14 August 2026, My team and I worked on the Laundry Pickup Pro project. I was required to keep an Agile logbook to keep track of what I had finished, what I planned to do next, and what was slowing me down. It also helped me stay organised and responsible for my own work, and it gave me a record of my progress that I could look back on when writing this reflection.

**Task:**
I had to use the logbook to keep myself in check. I had to write an honest entry every working day, set clear plans for what I will work on next, and be open about problems I am encountering or blockers that are preventing me from finishing what I planned for that particular day. I also had to manage my time well, because I had other school work to do. The logbook was meant to help me stay organised and take responsibility for my own progress.

**Action:**
Every day, I sat down and wrote my stand-up entry. Writing what I had finished showed me whether I was really making progress. Writing what I planned to do next gave me a clear goal for the following day, so I never started a day without knowing what to work on. Writing my problems down forced me to be honest about what was holding me back, like slow feedback, poor internet, a power outage, and the live location feature that would not update properly.

**Result:**
The logbook kept me responsible for my own work. I could not pretend I had made progress when I had not, because my entries showed the truth. It also helped me stay organised and avoid forgetting tasks. Because I wrote my problems down, I dealt with them sooner instead of letting them build up. By the end of the month, I had a full record of my progress that I could look back on and learn from. I learned that a daily check-in is a simple habit that keeps me honest and focused. I also learned that being open about problems makes them easier to solve.
